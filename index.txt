# Vedenism

*Justice, freedom, and peace. Heaven on earth, built by us.*

**Vedenism is a philosophical movement for justice, freedom, and peace.** It is open to people of any belief or none, and it is built on one idea: heaven on earth is not waited for, it is built.

---

## 1. Name and Symbol

**Vedenism** is **V + Eden + ism**. Eden is the garden of the old story, the picture of a world without cruelty. The **-ism** marks it as a living philosophy. The V stands at the start of the name and at the heart of the symbol.

Vedenism is a combination of many beliefs and philosophies. It draws on Stoicism, Buddhism, the Bible, Hellenic thought, and secular humanism, among others, and keeps only what protects equal worth, reduces harm, respects freedom of belief, and holds up to reason. Many ideas here echo older traditions, and you are welcome to trace them. Where an idea is Vedenism's own, we say so.

**The symbol** tells the whole philosophy in one picture:

- **The roots** hold a sword and scales. Justice is the foundation, and it has the strength to protect the vulnerable.
- **The tree** is Eden and the Tree of Life. Its trunk is twisted from two strands woven together: those who believe and those who do not, growing as one.
- **The V and the torch** carry a flame: freedom and the light of understanding.
- **The Greek key border** never breaks. It points to the Greek and Stoic roots, and to unity with no beginning and no end.

> Justice is the root. Eden is the tree. Freedom is the flame.

## 2. Origin

Vedenism did not begin in a single moment. It grew piece by piece.

It began as a child's anger at injustice. The founder saw how unfair the world could be and saw no one fixing it. He was mocked because his father was a welder and other families had government jobs. He decided not to wait for someone else. One day in high school, waiting for the school bell and looking at the sky through the window of the computing class, something sparked in him. He started designing a logically possible, just world: one with clear rules, equal dignity, and help for people in need.

Later, after the death of his pet bird, grieving and crying out to every god he could name, he burned a piece of paper on marble. It left a V with a line or dot above it that never came off. He chose the V for the name because of the mark. Whether it was a sign, a coincidence, or grief shaping what the eye wanted to see, we leave for each person to decide.

Later, working a job and short of money, he printed the first beta pages of his book, *The Path of Harmony: Teachings for a Better Life*, at his workplace. He never gave up on finishing it.

Vedenism is a philosophy. It welcomes people of any belief and requires none. It keeps learning from its own past, which is the principle it is built on.

> No paradise falls from the sky. Everything worth having is earned, by us or by those who came before us. If we want a world governed by justice, we must build it, one fair act at a time.

## Our Purpose

Vedenism began as a philosophy of self-reflection and self-improvement. Over time it grew to ask a larger question: what kind of world should people live in? Our answer is a world at peace, where countries promote justice and ethical rules and are not corrupted by greed or fear, where the vulnerable are protected, and where nature is cared for.

We start with ourselves, because a fair society is built by people who can question themselves and learn from their mistakes. Then we work outward, by showing a better model, speaking up for it, and helping in practice. Vedenism is the work of years and does not claim to be perfect. It claims to keep improving, and it invites anyone to help.

**What we offer.** Vedenism offers guidance and support for people seeking to live by goodness, justice, and personal growth. It promotes tolerance and respect for diversity, rejects harmful ideas, and aims to teach discipline, morality, handling emotions, and peaceful conflict resolution, so people can meet life's challenges with wisdom and compassion.

**Against racism and discrimination.** Racism and every form of discrimination break the first root: every soul has equal worth. One voice is easy to ignore, and many together are heard. Anyone who shares this goal, of any belief or none, is welcome to stand with us peacefully.

**Against corruption and harmful ideas, never against people.** We oppose corruption, coercion, and ideas that cause harm. We do not attack anyone for who they are.

**For the young.** Poverty should never decide who gets to learn. We advocate for a fair safety net for students and jobseekers, open to everyone on the same terms, and we make our case with evidence and peacefully.

**Guidance and courses.** Vedenism offers free courses, mentoring, and guidance on self-improvement, discipline, handling hardship, and learning from mistakes. This is education and peer support, not therapy or medical treatment. For mental health conditions, we encourage seeing a doctor or licensed counselor, and we can help you find one.

**Need help?** Vedenism is a philosophy and an educational project, not therapy, medical care, or an emergency service. If someone is in danger, contact your local emergency number right away. If you are struggling, tell someone you trust and consider a doctor or licensed counselor.

**Open doors.** There is no membership, no fee, and no obligation. Learning is free. Donations are voluntary, and they help us grow toward planting trees, giving away books, and supporting education.

## 3. Three Paths on Belief

Vedenism does not tell you what to believe about God. You may:

1. **Follow a religion.**
2. **Believe in God without a religion.**
3. **Not believe at all.**

All three are welcome, and none stands above the others. What unites us is not what we believe about heaven, but how we live on earth: with justice, freedom, and peace.

**We do not know what lies beyond what humans can perceive.** No one can prove or disprove God. So a Vedenist keeps three things apart: what we know, what we believe, and what we only speculate about.

**Scripture is wisdom, not authority.** Vedenism draws on Stoicism and the Bible alike, as sources of wisdom to be tested by reason.

**Goodness that does not depend on threats.** Fear of punishment after death weighs most on the gentle and the conscientious, while those who do real harm are rarely stopped by it. Vedenism asks for goodness that does not depend on threats, and holds that accountability for harm belongs to justice in this life.

**A just God would not wait to be asked** before helping the suffering, and neither should we. Vedenism measures a life not by its prayers but by its mercy. Vedenism does not require prayer or ritual. Those who pray are welcome to.

**What some Vedenists believe about the afterlife** (reincarnation, heaven, a self-made hell with unlocked doors, and others) is personal belief. It sits outside the core and binds no one.

## 4. Fixed Roots and Living Practice

Vedenism has two layers.

**Fixed roots (never change):**

1. Justice, freedom, and peace.
2. **Equal worth of every soul.** A soul is a soul, and humans are humans.
3. **No harm.** Freedom of belief and identity, for as long as no one is harmed.
4. **Freedom of conscience:** the right to choose, question, change your mind, criticize, and **leave**.
5. **No authority is exempt from scrutiny**, including the founder.
6. **Critical thinking and reason:** logic and evidence come first. Claims are judged by reasons, not by who says them or how old or popular they are. We keep apart what we know, what we believe, and what we only speculate about, and we question everything, including Vedenism itself.

**Living practice (open to change):** the list of universal wrongs, daily practices, customs, ceremonies, and how communities organize.

**Protecting the vulnerable.** Vedenism protects the believer and the doubter, the one who follows a religion and the one who follows none, the straight and the LGBTQ. No one should be judged, hunted, or silenced for living peacefully.

**Humility is for everyone.** Be whoever you are, openly and without fear. Do no harm, and do not use who you are to belittle others.

**Against corruption, not against belief.** Vedenism does not oppose religion or government. It opposes their corruption: coercion, exploitation, persecution, punishing people for leaving or doubting, and using fear or power to silence others. Any institution, sacred or secular, answers to the same test. Vedenism opposes oppression wherever it appears, and stands with the people who suffer under it, never against them.

**Peace is not passivity.** Vedenists answer harm with justice, not vengeance, and defend the vulnerable without becoming what they oppose. Self-defense against a real and immediate threat is allowed. Wars of conquest and holy wars are not. Try peace first, always.

**Culture.** Differences should dazzle, not be destroyed. Faith is for people, not for governing systems.

---

**Think clearly.** Logic examines our thinking, reason evaluates evidence and consequences, and self-improvement puts what we learn into practice. Science tells us what happens under a policy, but it does not decide every moral question, so we also need ethical reasoning about fairness, freedom, harm, and dignity. A simple test for any claim: *would I believe this if I had been born somewhere else?* *Think clearly. Examine honestly. Keep improving.*

## 5. Self-Discipline and Growth

A just world is built by people who keep working on themselves. Vedenism does not ask you to be perfect. It asks you to grow.

**Learning from mistakes.** When you make a mistake:

1. Recognize it.
2. Take responsibility.
3. Understand why it happened.
4. Repair the damage where you can.
5. Change what you do next time.
6. Keep improving.

Your past does not have to define you, but it should teach you. Do not strive to be perfect. Strive to be better.

**Self-discipline.** Discipline is choosing the right path over the easy one, again and again. It is not harshness toward yourself. Be strict with your habits and gentle with your worth.

**Hardship.** Hardship is part of life, and limits are what give effort its meaning. Endure it by feeling it, naming it, and then choosing your response. Step back from your emotions, then act. Good times show your character. Bad times show your strength. Asking for help is part of strength.

**Impermanence.** The only thing that is permanent is change. Good times and bad times both pass, so enjoy the first fully and trust that the second will end. Do not cling to what is temporary. Cherish it.

**Reason.** Use logic and evidence, ask questions, keep learning, and stay close to reality. Read, write, and question so ignorance does not rule you. Never let anyone's lack of knowledge lower their worth.

**The virtues:** wisdom, temperance, justice, courage, humility, and reason.

**A daily habit (optional):** each evening, ask yourself, *Did I act justly today? What did I learn? What will I do better tomorrow?*

## 6. Harm and the Universal Wrongs

**What counts as harm:** Harm is anything that damages a person's body, safety, freedom, or dignity, through violence, abuse, threats, harassment, coercion, manipulation, or deliberate cruelty.

**Mental harm** is deliberately damaging a person's peace, safety, or dignity through bullying, harassment, threats, humiliation, manipulation, coercion, stalking, or isolating them. Disagreement, criticism, honest hard truths, and being offended are **not** harm.

Three tests keep it fair:

1. **Intent or recklessness:** did they mean to hurt, or ignore obvious damage?
2. **Targeting:** is it aimed at a person, not at an idea or belief?
3. **Pattern or severity:** is it repeated or serious, not a single awkward remark?

**The Scale of Harm.** A way to see how serious a wrong is, from good to worst:

- **-1 Good deeds:** kindness, generosity, service
- **0 Neutral:** everyday life
- **1 Lapses:** small mistakes, to be learned from, not punished
- **2 Dishonesty and betrayal**
- **3 Harm:** violence, cruelty, neglect
- **4 Injustice:** discrimination, persecution, coercion
- **5 Abuse of power:** exploitation, oppression, slavery
- **6 Mass harm:** genocide, terrorism, mass murder

**The universal wrongs.** A wrong belongs on this list only if it harms someone or violates their freedom or dignity. Thoughts, feelings, private states, and beliefs are never wrongs. Only actions are.

*Harm to people*
1. Violence, except genuine self-defense or defending others from immediate harm
2. Murder and torture
3. Abuse, including domestic abuse and abuse of children
4. Any sexual act without consent, and any sexual act involving a child
5. Bullying and cruelty, physical or mental
6. Threats, stalking, and inciting violence
7. Recklessly endangering others, such as driving drunk

*Abuse of power and freedom*
8. Slavery, forced labor, and human trafficking
9. Coercion, including forced marriage and forced conversion
10. Exploiting the vulnerable
11. Discrimination or persecution because of race, gender, orientation, belief, or background
12. Wars of aggression, terrorism, and genocide
13. Corruption and abuse of office
14. Punishing people for leaving, doubting, or questioning

*Dishonesty and betrayal*
15. Slander and deliberate deception that harms (a lie that saves a life is not a wrong)
16. Theft, fraud, and extortion
17. Giving false witness
18. Betraying trust or breaking a serious promise

*The vulnerable and the earth*
19. Cruelty to animals
20. Neglecting those who depend on you, such as children and elders
21. Abandoning someone in need when you could easily help
22. Needless destruction of nature
23. Destroying cultural heritage or places of worship

If an act harms someone and is not named here, the general rule still applies: harm is a wrong. Harming yourself is not a wrong to punish. It calls for compassion and help.

**Repentance.** If you wrong someone, apologize and repair what you can. If you cannot, change your future actions. Only the person you wronged can forgive you.

**Seek your rights.** If someone wrongs you, do not stay silent.

**Rules prevent mistakes.** Unjust rules must be challenged peacefully.

## 7. Food and Body Care

*Living practice. Safety advice, not a commandment on diet. Food customs belong to each culture.*

Care for the body that carries you. Eat only food that is clean, fresh, and safe.

- Throw out moldy food immediately, without a second thought.
- Do not eat spoiled or infested food, or infested plants.
- Wild insects, snails, slugs, and other wild animals can carry parasites and diseases. Do not eat them unless they are properly prepared, cooked, and known to be safe.
- If you are not sure a plant, mushroom, or animal is safe, do not eat it. Research it first, or leave it.
- Never eat anything on a dare.
- Be cautious. Where food is grown, raised, and stored matters as much as the food itself.

Drinking, tattoos, and similar choices are fine in moderation and harmful in excess. Treat your body with respect, and treat your own pain with care, not shame.

## 8. Sharing the Ideas and the Amendment Rule

**Founder.** Vedenism was founded by Alexander Jacob Skyheart, and its words began as his own. No one, including the founder, stands above question. If you find a contradiction or harm in the founder's words, say so. It will be answered with reasons, and changed if you are right.

**Sharing.** Anyone may share Vedenism's ideas. No one who does so has special authority, and their words are tested by logic and care like anyone's. Share by explaining, not pressuring. Answer questions, accept "no" gracefully, and never harass, deceive, or shame anyone. Someone who does so does not speak for Vedenism.

**Not a cult, by design.** No fees, no required beliefs, no clergy, no secrets, and no obligation to stay. Anyone may question, criticize, or leave at any time, without penalty or shunning.

**No worship, no hierarchy.** Gratitude is welcome. Devotion to a person is not.

**Amendments.** Vedenists may reshape any living practice through honest discussion, evidence, and care for the vulnerable, as long as the roots remain. Any change must:

1. Protect equal worth.
2. Reduce harm, or at least add none.
3. Respect freedom of belief.
4. Be logical: consistent with the roots, backed by reasons or evidence, and free of contradiction.

It is judged by reasons, not by who proposes it. A proposal that seems illogical is answered with reasons, and the person may answer back.

Changes are proposed openly, discussed publicly, and judged by whether they reduce harm. If any teaching ever contradicts science, search for the truth and replace the error.

**Branches.** Vedenism is meant to be practiced in many ways, like the many schools of Stoicism. A Vedenist in a city, a village, or a century we cannot imagine may live it differently, and that is not a split but a sign it is alive. A branch is valid if it passes the test above.

> We plant the roots. Those who come after grow the branches.

**Gatherings.** Where people meet, doors are open and seats form a circle with no head. There is a library and a garden. People gather to learn, talk, celebrate, and help those in need. No fighting, hatred, or harsh language inside. Prayer or meditation is welcome for those who want it. Volunteers can listen and point people to professional help.

## 9. A Personal Pledge (optional)

> I will do no harm.
> I will treat every soul as equal in worth.
> I will seek justice, defend freedom, and keep the peace.
> I will question myself as I question others, and I will learn from my mistakes.
> Long live Vedenism, let there be peace and justice.

**Greeting (optional):** a hand over the heart, then slowly lowered.

| Page | What it holds |

### Social Skills (draft)

*Know your ground. Decide what you will accept and what you will not, and say so calmly and clearly. A limit is not an attack. It is information.*

*Speak to people the way you would speak to someone you care about: honestly, warmly, and without performing. Listen as though they matter, because they do.*

*Genuine does not mean careless. Kindness and honesty work together. Say the true thing in a way that respects the person hearing it.*

*Be open to others, and keep your ground. Without limits, openness can be used against you. Without openness, limits become walls.*
